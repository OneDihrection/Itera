### Description

```text
Tuntun helps patients and caregivers follow guided movement practice at home, one clear step at a time.

Choose hand and finger, arm, reaching, standing, or simple task practice such as folding a towel. Caregivers can set the side, repetitions, and rest time to match guidance from the patient's healthcare professional.

During a session, Tuntun shows movement examples and gives spoken and written cues. The iPad camera follows movement and counts observed repetitions. Pause, resume, or end a session when needed. Afterwards, review the session summary, practice history, and activity over time.

Recording choices let caregivers keep short video clips of selected repetitions, keep a movement skeleton only, or save no replay. By default, the app obscures faces and the background in saved clips. Automatic obscuring can miss identifying details; an unblurred option is also available with the patient's consent.

Practice data stays on the device. Tuntun does not require an account or send practice recordings to a developer-operated service. Data stored on the device may be included in iPad system backups.

Tuntun is a practice aid, not a diagnostic tool or a validated clinical measurement. Camera estimates can be affected by positioning, lighting, and occlusion. Follow the patient's clinician for a suitable plan and comfortable range; stop if movement is painful or unsafe.

Designed for iPad in landscape orientation. Available in Indonesian and English. Camera access is needed for movement tracking; voice controls require microphone and on-device speech recognition permission.
```

## App Review notes — English

```text
Tuntun is an iPad landscape app for guided movement practice. No account, payment, or external hardware is required. A caregiver may configure a plan before the patient starts.

For review: open Home, select a practice area, adjust the plan, and start a session. A physical iPad with a visible person or hand in camera view is needed to verify movement tracking and count a repetition. Camera permission is requested when starting camera-based practice. Voice control is optional; enabling it requests microphone and speech-recognition permission and uses on-device recognition. Sessions can also be controlled on screen. The app does not upload camera frames, audio, or session reports to a developer service.

Settings > Practice recordings offers Off, Skeleton only, and Video. Video defaults to obscuring both faces and background, but automated obscuring is imperfect. The caregiver may select face only, background only, or unblurred video after the in-app warning. Reports may include short clips of selected repetitions when Video is enabled.

Camera-derived repetitions and movement estimates are exercise aids, not validated clinical measurements. The app does not diagnose a condition or prescribe a rehabilitation dose. Practice plans should follow the patient's healthcare professional.
```

## App Privacy questionnaire — proposed answer

**Proposed selection: “No, we do not collect data from this app.”** Apple defines collection as transmission off the device in a way that lets the developer or a third party access data beyond a real-time request. The inspected app source contains no app network, analytics, advertising, account, or developer cloud integration. The app processes camera frames and optional voice commands on-device and stores plans, an optional patient name, summaries, activity history, and optional replays locally. Normal iPad system backups are separate from developer-operated collection. Recheck the final release binary and every integrated SDK before selecting this answer; a later upload, analytics SDK, support form, or service integration changes the response.
